A community-cat (TNR) rescue

When someone finds a litter of outdoor cats behind their apartment or workplace, they don't want cute animations—they need to know whether the kittens are old enough to trap, how to borrow a humane box trap, and which clinic takes ear-tip appointments tomorrow morning.

1. The Design Problem

Audience: First-time neighborhood colony caretakers, property managers, and trap-loan volunteers working out in the field at dawn or dusk.

TNR (Trap-Neuter-Return) has strict timing rules: you have to withhold food 24 hours before trapping, line the trap with newspaper, cover it with a breathable sheet to calm the cat, and never trap nursing mothers without locating the kittens. Previously, volunteers were texting paragraphs of instructions over SMS at 11 PM because the website didn't have a clear checklist.

2. What I Built

I structured the entire homepage like a rugged field manual: a numbered 5-step TNR timeline ('1. Assess & Count → 2. Book Clinic Spot → 3. Trap Loan & Bait → 4. Spay/Neuter + Ear Tip → 5. Recovery & Return'), a visual 'Found Kittens? Wait Before You Scoop' age decision tree, and a trap-loan request block that works on old browsers without a single line of client JS.

3. What Was Tricky

TNR instructions are dense, and long walls of text cause people to miss critical safety steps (like keeping traps indoors overnight after anesthesia so cats can regulate their body temperature). I used Swiss-grid callout boxes with high-contrast warning stripes (`Warning: Post-Op Temperature Rule`) and semantic `<details>` accordions so caretakers can scan the 5 steps in 15 seconds or expand the exact step they're standing in front of.
