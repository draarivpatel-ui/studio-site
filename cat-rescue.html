A no-kill cat rescue

No-kill rescues take in senior, diabetic, and injured cats that municipal shelters can't treat, which means vet bills are their biggest bottleneck. I designed this site around big, honest portrait photography and a persistent, transparent medical-fund donation path.

1. The Design Problem

Audience: Recurring monthly donors, local cat adopters, and clinic sponsors checking how medical donations are spent.

Donors are much more generous when they know what their money actually buys ($25 covers microchip + rabies vaccine; $120 covers a dental extraction), but the rescue's old site only had a generic PayPal button buried in the footer. Meanwhile, special-needs and senior cats sat at the bottom of the page with tiny thumbnails.

2. What I Built

I created a sticky header donate trigger paired with concrete 'What your gift covers' cost-breakdown cards ($25, $50, $120, $250) and a spotlight rail for senior and bonded-pair cats. The photography grid uses progressive responsive image sizing so full-bleed cat portraits look crisp on Retina displays while staying under a strict 16 KB HTML+CSS budget.

3. What Was Tricky

Sticky donate bars on phones can easily feel pushy or cover up content when someone is trying to read a cat's medical bio. I tuned the mobile bar to sit cleanly in the top header with a compact pill button (`min-height: 44px`) and built the cost-breakdown cards using pure CSS grid so nothing overlaps or janks during scroll.

Accessibility & QA Checks

  • Every donation amount card exposes its full description to screen readers ('Donate $50 - covers initial bloodwork and FIV/FeLV test for one intake').
  • No auto-playing carousels or background video: all cat spotlights sit in a keyboard-focusable static grid.
  • Tested with macOS VoiceOver and Safari Reader mode to ensure every cat's age, spay/neuter status, and adoption fee reads in logical DOM order.
