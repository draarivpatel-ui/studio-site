A community food pantry

Built for neighbors looking up distribution hours, bus routes, and eligibility rules—often on a prepaid phone with spotty data while standing outside or riding transit. Clarity, dignity, and large type come before everything else.

1. The Design Problem

Audience: Neighbors seeking groceries for their household, plus community volunteers checking shift times and most-needed shelf-stable donations.

On too many food pantry sites, the distribution hours are trapped inside a scanned PDF flyer or buried below a 600-word mission statement for donors. If someone is stressed and trying to find out whether they need a photo ID or utility bill before the pantry closes at 6 PM, making them pinch-zoom a PDF is a design failure.

2. What I Built

I put the live weekly schedule, street address, transit directions, and a bold 'No ID or proof of income required—everyone is welcome' banner in the top 320 pixels of the viewport. Right underneath is a four-step 'What happens on your first visit' walkthrough written at a 6th-grade reading level so first-time visitors know where to park, that volunteers provide boxes, and that they get to choose their own groceries.

3. What Was Tricky

The pantry serves two completely different audiences: neighbors picking up food (who need hours and zero friction) and donors/volunteers (who need drop-off windows and a list of high-priority pantry staples like cooking oil and canned protein). I separated the two flows right in the top bar so donor fundraising appeals never get in the way of someone looking for food.

Accessibility & QA Checks

  • Base body font size is 18px with 1.65 line-height; schedule tables use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` and never scroll horizontally at 320px.
  • Zero-stigma language review: removed bureaucratic terms like 'beneficiaries' and 'intake verification' in favor of 'neighbors' and 'first visit'.
  • High-contrast forest green (`#183A2C`) on warm alabaster (`#F9F8F4`) achieves 12.1:1 contrast for outdoor afternoon readability.
