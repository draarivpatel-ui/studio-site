A foster-based cat rescue

A warm, phone-first site for an all-volunteer foster cat network. Designed around the reality that 82% of their visitors arrive from Instagram or Facebook links on a phone and decide within ten seconds whether to apply to foster, browse cats, or donate.

1. The Design Problem

Audience: Prospective fosters and adopters browsing from mobile social feeds, often during a commute or evening scroll.

Because the rescue doesn't have a physical shelter building, visitors kept asking where they could 'visit the cats' and dropping off before reading how foster-to-adopt works. Their old link-tree style page split donations, foster applications, and cat bios across four mismatched third-party forms.

2. What I Built

I designed an editorial card layout that explains the 3-step foster-to-adopt loop right below the hero before showing the adoptable cat grid. Each cat card uses explicit width/height attributes to eliminate layout shift (CLS = 0.00) and badges medical/temperament tags ('Good with dogs', 'Bonded pair', 'FIV+') in plain text rather than tiny icons.

3. What Was Tricky

Volunteer coordinators needed to update cat availability without touching layout code, and foster photos come in every aspect ratio imaginable. I built a CSS aspect-ratio frame (`4 / 3` with `object-fit: cover` and focal-point utility classes) plus a clean JSON-to-HTML card partial so cropping never decapitates ear tips or distorts portraits.

Accessibility & QA Checks

  • Every adoptable cat card uses a single composite link with an explicit `aria-label` ('Meet Mochi - 2-year-old tabby, good with other cats') instead of three redundant 'Learn More' buttons.
  • Warm cream canvas (`#FBF8F3`) paired with deep espresso ink (`#1F1A17`) hits 14.8:1 contrast, easy on the eyes without sterile pure-white glare.
  • Donation tier buttons work as native radio labels with full keyboard arrow navigation and 52px height on mobile.
